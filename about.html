Earth Jukebox

Hear the planet. Every sound comes from the NASA picture, at the place it happened.

Not an official NASA product. A sonification of NASA visualizations, not of the raw data. Values read from colors are approximate.

The problem

NASA's Earth Information Center shows the planet through beautiful moving pictures: lightning, city lights, ocean currents, smoke and dust. All of it is visual. People who are blind or have low vision get, at best, a sentence of description. The challenge asked for an interface that pairs these frames with sound generated in real time, so the pictures can be experienced and explored by ear.

What it does

  • Plays the picture. Pick a record; the browser reads every frame of the NASA video about 15 times a second and turns what it sees into music on a steady beat.
  • Explore by touch. Move a finger, mouse, or the arrow keys over the map and hear what is under it: the instrument says what it is, the pulse says how much, pitch and stereo say where. Press D for a spoken description. Works while playing and while paused.
  • See the sound. Every note draws a ripple on the map where it came from, in its instrument's color.
  • Learn the sounds plays each instrument with its name first. Captions describe the music every few seconds.
  • Live Earth. "Earth Today" plays yesterday's whole planet from NASA GIBS; current natural events from NASA EONET appear as markers the probe can touch.

How the mapping works

One sound language for every record:

SoundMeans
Which instrumentWhat it is (lightning, warm water, dust…)
Left / rightWhere it is across the map
Pitch, snapped to a pentatonic scaleHow high it is on the map
Loudness, brightness, pulse speedHow much of it there is
RecordThe engine readsYou hear
Lightning (SVS 5011)New yellow lightning dotsA drum hit per flash; a rumble that grows with the flash rate
Night Lights (SVS 5313)Lit places and their change colorA sweep of plucks: bright pluck for light and more light, soft pluck for less light
Ocean currents (SVS 3912)Warm vs cold water; motionA warm pad and a cold bell; a melody that follows the fastest water
Aerosols (SVS 5552)Dust, smoke, sea-salt colorsGritty noise, a low breathy rush, an airy hiss
B-sides and Earth TodayBrightness, strongest color, motionLoudness, chord, tempo

Every color range was measured from the clips with a calibration tool, not guessed. The same frame always gives the same sound; nothing is random.

Who it helps

People who are blind or have low vision, first: everything works by keyboard and screen reader, and the map can be explored by touch on a phone. It also helps anyone who learns better by listening, and visitors to a gallery or classroom who want to hear what a visualization is saying.

Test results

Not yet run. We will report the listening test and screen-reader test here exactly as they happen, including how many people took part.

Honest limits

  • The engine reads rendered pictures, not the scientific data, so amounts are approximate.
  • B-sides with a moving camera are artistic, not geographic: left and right do not stay on one place.
  • EONET alerts are not a warning system and can lag.
  • Raga scale presets are experimental and not yet approved by a musician.
