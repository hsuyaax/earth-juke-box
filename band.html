Jukebox Earth Band How it works

Earth Band (beta)

NASA visualizations play together as one band, each read live from its own video, on one beat and in one key: lightning on drums, ocean currents on bass, night lights on lead, CO2 on the harmony pad, aerosols on shaker, Perpetual Ocean on arpeggio, forest height on marimba, the atmospheric river on strings, air quality on the drone. Up to four play at once.

The data decides when, where, how much, and which way the harmony moves. Music theory decides which exact notes are allowed. Every note comes from a frame; the pads only switch parts on, off, or thinner.

An artistic remix. These visualizations cover different years and are played together, not as simultaneous events.

Only the bass is on to start. Switch on the other members to add them.

Quick start

Pads

Tap a pad to switch that part on or off. Hold it to hear it alone, and let go to return. With a keyboard: arrow keys move, Space or Enter switches, holding Space or pressing S solos. Escape stops all sound.

Sparse thins the part you last touched.

What each member plays

    The key is A minor. The lead keeps to A minor pentatonic, and on beats 1 and 3 it moves to a tone of the current chord when one is within two scale steps. Chords change on bar lines in four-bar phrases that always start on A minor and lead back to it; how active the switched-on members are decides which way the phrase goes (calm: A minor, F, C, G; busy: A minor, D minor, E minor, E minor). The bass takes its warm share scaled to the range it actually covers in this clip (35.7 to 40.0 percent). Lightning, currents, night lights and aerosols are flat maps, so left and right still mean where. CO2, Perpetual Ocean, forest height, the atmospheric river and air quality have moving cameras, so they use whole-picture features and sit at fixed places in the stereo field. A member's video loads the first time you switch it on and plays only while it is on.

    Not an official NASA product. A sonification of NASA visualizations, not of the raw data; values read from colors are approximate.

    Visualizations: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (5011, 5313, 5196, 5552 and 5669, 5425, 4950, 4960, 5533); NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio (3912). Clips reduced in size and frame rate for the band. All sound is synthesized in your browser.
